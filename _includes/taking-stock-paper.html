Abstract

We provide causal evidence on the effects of equity-based CEO incentive pay on corporate innovation activity. To capture exogenous variation in equity-based pay, we exploit a large sample of public-company relative performance share plans under which realized equity payouts vary discontinuously around performance cutoffs. Using a regression discontinuity research design, we find that CEO stock award payouts lead to significant increases in a firm’s patenting activity. Moreover, these stock award payouts increase the originality, quality, and value of a firm’s patents. We also document that payout-induced increases in innovation activity are more pronounced in the presence of managerial short-termism or restrictions on selling, hedging, or pledging of shares. Overall, our findings show that equity-based pay for top management is an important driver of corporate innovation.
